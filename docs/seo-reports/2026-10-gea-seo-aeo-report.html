Read first: what this run could not check

The cloud environment's network policy blocked outbound access to grantsea.com.au, google.com and the PageSpeed API for every tool, confirmed by a 403 at the egress proxy. Every check that needs a live page (structured data as served, debug-route status codes, robots.txt and sitemap as served, title and meta tags, Core Web Vitals, suburb page content, the homepage rating strip) is marked Not checked with that reason.

The audit instead reads the Next.js source in the grantsea-website repository. That is a reliable proxy for what ships to production, but not a substitute for a live check. The live portion should be re-run from an environment that can reach the site, or supplied manually next cycle.

Our recommendation

Fix the offices page and the homepage stats before anything else

Three fabricated office addresses and four placeholder hero statistics are live on production. They are the cheapest fixes in this report and the ones that most directly undermine the trust signals Google and AI answers rely on.

What changed this month

Confirmed Google changes

September 2026 spam update. Google began rolling this out globally on 24 September, warning it could take up to two weeks, its longest spam-update rollout yet. It is the fourth confirmed spam update of 2026 after March, June and August. Google has not disclosed the tactic it targets. No action for GEA beyond the standing advice to avoid thin or duplicate content; see the suburb page finding. Sources: Search Engine Journal, ppc.land.

VideoObject structured data, 24 September. Google added a creator property and clarified interactionStatistic support. Not urgent for GEA, which uses no video schema today, but relevant if property video tours get structured data later. Source: Search Engine Roundtable.

Reported but not verified

AI-generated search summaries this month repeatedly mentioned a "September 2026 core update" and a "product reviews update". No clearly dated primary article from Search Engine Journal, Search Engine Land or Google could be confirmed for either. One Search Engine Watch headline was phrased speculatively, which does not fit a settled update. Google's own status dashboard was unreachable this run. Check it manually before treating either as real.

Google's "SAFE" anti-spam system (Scaled Abuse Forensics Examiner), aimed at AI-generated spam networks, was widely discussed this month, but the underlying documentation dates to around May to June 2026. This is wider awareness of an existing system, not a September deployment.

AI assistant citation behaviour

No rigorous, real estate specific study on how ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Perplexity cite local businesses was published in this window. The nearest in-window data point is a vendor (Uberall) analysis of about 120,000 AI mentions, reportedly finding Gemini surfaces around eight times more unique local businesses than ChatGPT. Directional only, and the publication could not be located, so treat it as low to moderate confidence. BrightLocal's 2026 survey (45 percent of consumers now use AI to find local businesses, up from 6 percent) and Semrush's 2026 AI Visibility Index are credible but earlier in the year and should not be re-cited as this month's news.

Standing position. There is no ranking system inside ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Perplexity. They produce generative answers, not a ranked index you can optimise into. Several of this month's results were affiliate listicles and PR-distributed "studies" promising to rank a business first in ChatGPT via an undisclosed "AI visibility score". They are excluded from the recommendations.

Australian real estate

No Elite Agent, REB or Stepps article specifically on search or AI visibility for Australian agents was published in this window. The nearest relevant pieces, Elite Agent's Be the Answer and The new #1 ranking, and REB's How AI is reshaping SEO, date from March to May 2026. Stepps' AI content is notable for warning against vendors overselling guaranteed ChatGPT placement.
