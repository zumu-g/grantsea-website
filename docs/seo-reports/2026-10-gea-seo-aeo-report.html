What changed this month

Confirmed Google changes

September 2026 spam update (confirmed). Google began rolling this out globally on 24 September, warning it could take up to two weeks — its longest spam-update rollout yet. It's the fourth confirmed spam update of 2026 (after March, June and August) and Google hasn't disclosed what tactic it specifically targets. No action is indicated for GEA beyond the standing advice to avoid thin/duplicate content — see the suburb-page finding below. Sources: Search Engine Journal, ppc.land.

VideoObject structured-data update (24 September). Google added a creator property and clarified interactionStatistic support for VideoObject schema. Not urgent for GEA today (no video schema is in use), but worth keeping in mind if property video tours get structured data later. Source: Search Engine Roundtable.

Reported but not independently verified

AI-generated search summaries this month repeatedly mentioned a "September 2026 core update" and a "product reviews update," but no single, clearly-dated primary article from Search Engine Journal, Search Engine Land or Google itself could be confirmed for either — one Search Engine Watch headline on the topic was phrased speculatively ("Could Google's next core update arrive in September 2026?"), which is inconsistent with a settled update. status.search.google.com could not be reached this session to check Google's own record directly — recommend a manual check next cycle before treating either as real.

Google's new anti-spam system, "SAFE" (Scaled Abuse Forensics Examiner), aimed at AI-generated spam networks, was newly discussed in the trade press this month (Search Engine Watch), but commentators note the underlying documentation dates to roughly May–June 2026 — so this is wider awareness of an existing system, not a September deployment.

AI-assistant citation behaviour

No rigorous, independently verifiable, real-estate-specific study on how ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini/Perplexity cite local businesses was published in this exact 30-day window. The closest in-window data point is a vendor (Uberall) analysis of roughly 120,000 AI mentions, reportedly finding Gemini surfaces around 8× more unique local businesses than ChatGPT — directional only, and its own publication could not be independently located, so treat it as low-to-moderate confidence. Two broader, credible studies exist but fall outside the window and should not be re-cited as "this month's" news: BrightLocal's 2026 survey (45% of consumers now use AI to find local businesses, up from 6%) and Semrush's 2026 AI Visibility Index (126M AI prompts analysed) — both published earlier in 2026.

Per standing policy for this report: there is no literal ranking system inside ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Perplexity the way there is in Google Search — these produce generative answers, not a ranked index you can "optimise into." A number of this month's search results were SEO-content-farm/affiliate listicles and PR-distributed "studies" promising to get a business to "rank #1 in ChatGPT" via an undisclosed "AI visibility score." These are marketing hype, not credible technique, and are excluded from the recommendations below.

Offices page — address accuracy (NAP) Critical. All three offices in src/app/offices/page.tsx use fabricated placeholder addresses: Narre Warren "123 Main Street," Berwick "456 High Street," Pakenham "789 Prince Highway" — each with matching fake Google Maps links built from the same placeholder text, and manager names (Sarah Thompson, Michael Chen, Emma Wilson) that could not be verified as real. Replace with real, verified addresses, suite numbers, managers and Google Maps links for all three offices before any further local-SEO work on this page. Stuart to confirm correct details.
NAP consistency — org vs offices vs contact The sitewide org schema uses one HQ phone number (+61 1300 472 687) and a single Berwick address. The /offices page shows three different local phone numbers per office. This isn't necessarily wrong (a head-office number plus branch lines is normal), but it couldn't be cross-checked against the live /contact page or Google Business Profile this session. Once live-site access is restored, confirm all phone numbers and addresses match exactly across schema, /offices, /contact and each office's Google Business Profile.
Agent profile content (Q&A / reviews) src/data/agentProfiles.ts: AGENT_PROFILES is an empty object — 0 agents have real Q&A or reviews populated. Every agent currently falls back to placeholderQA(), which renders clearly-marked placeholder text ("[Placeholder — awaiting <name>'s answer...]"). To the codebase's credit, this is handled responsibly — placeholders are visibly marked rather than disguised, and the file's own comments note fabricated reviews are an ACL legal risk, so the reviews section stays hidden until genuine quotes exist. Collect real Q&A answers and verified reviews from agents, starting with the highest-listing-volume agents, and add them to agentProfiles.ts.
Homepage proof stats src/app/page-oncom-style.tsx (~L1898–1956) hardcodes "250+ Properties sold monthly," "4.9 Average rating," "98% Success rate" and "15+ Expert agents" as static JSX with no fetch or computation nearby. This directly contradicts the repo's own NO_MOCK_DATA_POLICY.md, which explicitly lists "placeholder responses that appear real" as never allowed. Notably, a real, live Google rating is already wired up elsewhere (getGoogleReviews(), used on /sell and /reviews with 6-hour ISR) — the homepage tile just doesn't use it. Replace the hero stat tile with real numbers: reuse getGoogleReviews() for the rating, compute agent count from the live roster, and either substantiate or remove "properties sold monthly" and "success rate."
